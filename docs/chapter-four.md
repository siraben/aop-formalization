# Chapter 4: relations

This inventory covers all 52 numbered exercises in Chapter 4. [Relations](../src/AoP/Relations.agda) represents an edge by a witness and a composite by two edges with a shared intermediate point. For example, two Boolean-valued choices can lead to the same final output through different intermediate values. Exercise 4.3 shows exactly where that difference matters.

For Proposition 4.1 in concrete `Rel`, [InjectionAdjunction](../src/AoP/InjectionAdjunction.agda) identifies the right adjoint `S` with the converse of `R`. [AdjointGraph](../src/AoP/AdjointGraph.agda) extracts the function: at each input the unit supplies a round trip, whose intermediate point is the output. The counit forces every other output there to equal that one. `sound` and `complete` prove that the extracted function’s graph and `R` contain exactly the same edges.

## §4.3: tabulations

[TabulationMediator](../src/AoP/TabulationMediator.agda) proves Proposition 4.2 in concrete `Rel` for any supplied table. The span with columns `h,k` is included in `R` exactly when each of its inputs can be mapped to a table row with those column values. `factor` constructs the row from inclusion; `include` reads it back as a witness of `R`. If the table columns jointly distinguish rows, any two such maps agree pointwise (`unique`). [TabulationMeet](../src/AoP/TabulationMeet.agda) checks the intermediate relation `(f˘ ∘ h) ⊓ (g˘ ∘ k)` on function graphs. A candidate row must pass both column tests. Joint monicity makes it single-valued before any existence assumption; span inclusion supplies a matching row for each input. `graph-in` and `graph-out` identify the intersection with the graph of the constructed mediator.

[TabulationIso](../src/AoP/TabulationIso.agda) compares two tables of the same relation. Use each table’s columns as a span into the other to get `encode` and `decode`. Each round trip preserves both column values; joint monicity therefore recovers the original row. `unique` proves pointwise uniqueness among maps that preserve both columns.

[RelUnitObject](../src/AoP/RelUnitObject.agda) characterizes the unit objects of concrete `Rel`: a type must have an inhabitant and any two inhabitants must be equal. An entire relation from the singleton supplies the inhabitant; testing the scalar law on the full endorelation forces equality. Conversely, the constant map to that inhabitant covers every input. Its converse composed with the corresponding constant map on another type contains every relation between the two types: the chosen inhabitant witnesses every path. [UnitTerminalBridge](../src/AoP/UnitTerminalBridge.agda) proves that this unit condition is equivalent to terminality in `Fun`. For the reverse implication, take any edge of a relation on the terminal type. The two columns of its table are functions from the same row type to the terminal type, so they agree at the row for that edge. Its endpoints must therefore coincide.

[FullTabulationProduct](../src/AoP/FullTabulationProduct.agda) shows why tabulating the full relation gives a product in `Fun`. For any functions `h` and `k` on a common input, their observed pair lies in the full relation. Proposition 4.2 therefore supplies a row map with projections `h` and `k`; joint monicity makes it unique. `terminal` packages this argument as the terminal-span property of a product from §2.5. For the ordinary pair type, `Pairs.columns` and `Pairs.paired` prove both inclusions in the final calculation: the graph of `d ↦ (h d, k d)` is precisely the intersection of the two column tests.

[Tabulation](../src/AoP/Tabulation.agda) gives a table one row per witnessed edge. Reading its source and target columns reconstructs the relation. Joint injectivity of the columns follows when evidence for any given edge is unique.

## §4.4: local completeness and Galois connections

[ResidualFromJoin](../src/AoP/ResidualFromJoin.agda) derives the universal property shared by implication and division. Fix the right operand of meet or composition and call the resulting operation `apply`. Join all candidates `x` satisfying `apply x ≤ s`. Each admissible candidate lies below that join. Conversely, distributing `apply` over the join shows that the join itself is admissible, since every summand is bounded by `s`. Monotonicity gives the same bound for anything below the join. The formal statement assumes small carriers and the indicated indexed joins. [ImplicationSingletonJoin](../src/AoP/ImplicationSingletonJoin.agda) specializes the argument to implication in `Rel`: admit the singleton at `(b,a)` precisely when intersecting it with `R` gives only edges of `S`. `largest` proves that every edge of every admissible test contributes such a singleton. `into` and `out` identify their join with the pointwise implication `R b a → S b a`, without deciding either predicate.

[DivisionSingletonJoin](../src/AoP/DivisionSingletonJoin.agda) constructs this join for left division in concrete `Rel` using singleton relations. Admit the singleton at `(a,b)` when composing it with `R` gives only edges of `S`. Every edge of any admissible relation contributes such a singleton, and their join is still admissible. The index consists of two endpoints and their admissibility witness, so the construction uses only a small join. `into` and `out` prove equality with the usual fibre-containment test.

[MeetFromJoin](../src/AoP/MeetFromJoin.agda) constructs the meet in exercise 4.28 for a small ordered carrier: take the join of all common lower bounds, then verify both clauses of the greatest-lower-bound property.

[RangeJoins](../src/AoP/RangeJoins.agda) proves exercise 4.29 for small indexed families in `Rel`: taking range commutes with union because the input witness and the branch index can be supplied in either order.

[RelDifference](../src/AoP/RelDifference.agda) checks exercise 4.30 for concrete relations. The universal property uses decidability of membership in the subtracted relation to recover a cover. The four difference identities record where such a decision is needed.

[EmptyRange](../src/AoP/EmptyRange.agda) checks exercise 4.31 in `Rel`: a relation is empty exactly when its range is empty. Exchanging the two input witnesses around a triangle then proves that `(R ∘ S) ⊓ T` is empty exactly when `R ⊓ (T ∘ S˘)` is empty.

[RelImplication](../src/AoP/RelImplication.agda) checks exercise 4.32 in `Rel`. At each pair of endpoints, implication turns an `R` witness into an `S` witness. The proof gives its universal property and both inclusions for each of the four identities.

[ImplicationPullback](../src/AoP/ImplicationPullback.agda) proves exercise 4.33 for function graphs: changing both endpoints before forming an implication gives the same relation as changing them afterwards.

[LexicalOrder](../src/AoP/LexicalOrder.agda) checks exercise 4.34 in `Rel`. Compare by `R`, consulting `S` when `R` ties. This operation preserves preorders, is associative, and has the universal relation as a two-sided unit. [LexicalPairs](../src/AoP/LexicalPairs.agda) proves that the worked example on natural-number pairs agrees with the strict-first-or-tied comparison. It checks `(1,9) ≤ (2,0)` by the first coordinate and `(2,1) ≤ (2,3)` by the second.

[LeftDivision](../src/AoP/LeftDivision.agda) checks the universal property of left division in `Rel`. Exercise 4.35 moves a function graph across either argument of division; the graph fixes the intermediate input, giving both directions of each equality. [DivisionCalculation](../src/AoP/DivisionCalculation.agda) checks the preceding three unnumbered identities for a composite, a union on the left, and a meet on the right.

[ResidualConnections](../src/AoP/ResidualConnections.agda) checks exercise 4.36: fixing either factor makes composition left adjoint to division. Subtraction is left adjoint to adjoining the subtracted relation when its membership is decidable. Both maps in each pair are monotone by the adjunction laws.

[OppositeComposition](../src/AoP/OppositeComposition.agda) checks exercise 4.37: the proposed adjunction holds exactly when the two round trips satisfy `X ⊆ S ∘ (X ∘ R)` and `(S ∘ Y) ∘ R ⊆ Y` for all `X` and `Y`.

[FloorConnection](../src/AoP/FloorConnection.agda) checks the order argument for exercise 4.38. Integer inclusion into the rationals is the lower adjoint and floor the upper adjoint: `embed z ≤ q` exactly when `z ≤ floor q`. The proof assumes the greatest-integer-below specification and derives monotonicity; [MixedFloorAdjunction](../src/AoP/MixedFloorAdjunction.agda) proves the equivalence on each fixed positive-denominator grid using signed whole parts and bounded natural remainders. For instance, −9/8 is represented by −2 + 7/8 and has floor −2. [RationalFloorOrder](../src/AoP/RationalFloorOrder.agda) allows different positive denominators: it orders proper fractions by inclusion of their rational lower cuts and compares signed whole parts first. It proves reflexivity, transitivity, and both directions of the embedding/floor adjunction. [FractionCrossOrder](../src/AoP/FractionCrossOrder.agda) proves that lower-cut inclusion for the fractional parts is equivalent to the usual cross-product inequality. Testing inclusion at the left fraction gives necessity; scaling a probe inequality and cancelling a positive denominator gives sufficiency.

[MeetConnection](../src/AoP/MeetConnection.agda) checks exercise 4.39: the diagonal map is left adjoint to binary meet in the componentwise product order. It derives the adjunction from the meet projections and pairing, recovers both laws, and proves monotonicity of the two maps.

[AdjointJoins](../src/AoP/AdjointJoins.agda) checks both directions of exercise 4.40 for monotone `f` on orders with small indexed joins. Given an adjunction, `f` preserves joins and `g y` is the join of the inputs whose images lie below `y`. Conversely, join preservation makes that join itself admissible, yielding `f x ≤ y` exactly when `x ≤ g y`.

## §4.5: Boolean allegories

[DivisionStability](../src/AoP/DivisionStability.agda) gives the exact hypothesis on a fixed numerator `X` for equation (4.21) in concrete `Rel`. [Complement](../src/AoP/Complement.agda) proves that stability of `X` (each edge follows from its double negation) suffices for every denominator. Conversely, take the denominator to be identity. Any counterexample path must then end at its starting input; refuting that path gives double negation of the same `X` edge. The reverse inclusion at identity turns this into an actual edge. Thus requiring the equation uniformly in the denominator already forces stability of `X`.

[NegationLaws](../src/AoP/NegationLaws.agda) checks exercises 4.41–4.42 in `Rel`. Negating the universal relation gives the empty relation, triple negation reduces to single negation, negation exchanges union with intersection of complements, and excluded middle holds under double negation. Eliminating double negation for all relations is equivalent to deciding membership in all relations. [Schroeder](../src/AoP/Schroeder.agda) proves exercise 4.43 for concrete relations: a path inclusion rules out each forbidden step, and stability of the target relation suffices for the reverse implications. [ModularDisjointness](../src/AoP/ModularDisjointness.agda) proves the `R˘ ∘ ¬T ⊑ ¬S` form in an abstract allegory. The modular law moves an overlap inside a composite, where composition with zero annihilates it. `contract` and `expand` rotate that disjointness test without negation; `forward` introduces the complement and `backward` uses stability of `T`. [RightModularDisjointness](../src/AoP/RightModularDisjointness.agda) derives the other rotation, `¬T ∘ S˘ ⊑ ¬R`. Taking converses preserves disjointness: reversing a common lower bound gives a lower bound in the original hom-order. Apply the first rotation to the reversed path and turn it back. Rotating twice recovers the original path bound under the same stability assumption on `T`.

## §4.6: power allegories

[TransposeColumnTests](../src/AoP/TransposeColumnTests.agda) checks the two-division formula for transpose in concrete `Rel`. A candidate subset `X` represents the outputs of `R` at `b` exactly when every member of `X` is an output and every output belongs to `X`. These are the two column tests obtained by dividing membership by `R` and vice versa. Taking both columns from membership itself yields subset antisymmetry for extensional equality of predicates. [TabularIndirectEquality](../src/AoP/TabularIndirectEquality.agda) formalizes the indirect proof principle in an abstract allegory: to prove `X ⊑ Y`, it suffices to transfer every span below `X` to one below `Y`, provided `X` itself factors as such a span. Apply the transfer to the two legs of that factorization. For equality, apply the argument in both directions. Joint monicity is unnecessary for this step. [SpanColumnShunt](../src/AoP/SpanColumnShunt.agda) checks a step of the abstract calculation: `mem ∘ (f ∘ g˘) ⊑ R` exactly when `mem ∘ f ⊑ R ∘ g`. The unit of the map `g` inserts a round trip in one direction; its counit removes one in the other. The proof does not require `mem` or `R` to be maps. [SpanDivisionTests](../src/AoP/SpanDivisionTests.agda) combines both tests. On the span `f ∘ g˘`, passing the first division means `mem ∘ f ⊑ R ∘ g`. Reversing the span swaps its legs, so the second division gives the opposite inclusion. `read` combines them into equality of the observed columns; `write` recovers both tests from that equality. [TabularTransposeFormula](../src/AoP/TabularTransposeFormula.agda) completes the abstract calculation. On each test span, transpose cancellation identifies the column of `transpose ∘ g` with `R ∘ g`; uniqueness of a map with that membership column then identifies it with `f`. `formula` applies the two span transfers to factorizations of the transpose and the intersection. Its hypotheses supply the map unit and counit, closure under composition, division laws, and membership cancellation and uniqueness. [AllegoryMapOrder](../src/AoP/AllegoryMapOrder.agda) derives the inclusion-to-equality step: if an entire `f` lies below a simple `g`, insert `f˘ ∘ f` after `g`, enlarge `f˘` to `g˘`, then cancel `g ∘ g˘`. This gives `g ⊑ f`, so the two arrows agree. For the usual maps (entire and simple arrows), [AllegoryMapComposition](../src/AoP/AllegoryMapComposition.agda) also proves closure under composition. For the composite’s unit, insert the second map’s unit into the first round trip; for its counit, contract the first map’s counit inside the second. `Canonical.Theorem` applies the transpose calculation to these maps without a separate closure or discreteness assumption. `Canonical.Subsets.antisymmetric` specializes the formula to membership itself: identity already satisfies transpose cancellation, so mutual subset inclusion lies below identity. The specialization uses factorizations of identity and of the mutual-inclusion test.

[PowerTransposeTerminal](../src/AoP/PowerTransposeTerminal.agda) gives the categorical meaning of the power transpose. Fix the output `A`. Objects are relations into `A`; an arrow changes the input by a function while preserving the relation. If membership is terminal here, the unique arrow from `R` sends each input to its set of `R`-outputs: its commuting triangle gives cancellation, and terminal uniqueness forces that choice. Conversely, cancellation and uniqueness of transpose make membership terminal. `Derived.reflection` takes the transpose of membership itself: the identity already satisfies its triangle. `Derived.fusion` precomposes a relation with a function graph; the resulting change of input composes with the original terminal map, so transposing before or after that change gives the same arrow. The construction takes the candidate power object and membership arrow as inputs; it does not assume that all powersets live in the input universe.

[ExistentialImageFunctor](../src/AoP/ExistentialImageFunctor.agda) derives the existential image from that same universal property. With membership supplied at each output object, define `E R = Λ (R ∘ mem)`. To prove `E R ∘ Λ S ≈ Λ (R ∘ S)`, read membership after the left side: the first cancellation exposes `R ∘ mem`, and the second recovers `S`. Choosing `S` itself as a relation after membership gives preservation of composition. Reflection supplies the identity law. [ImageSingletonNaturality](../src/AoP/ImageSingletonNaturality.agda) restricts existential image to function graphs to form the powerset functor. Membership is natural by transpose cancellation. For singleton, both paths through the square transpose the same function graph: applying image after singleton uses absorption, while taking the singleton after the function uses transpose fusion. This square ranges over functions.

[ImageUnionLaws](../src/AoP/ImageUnionLaws.agda) takes the existential image of membership as union. Flattening a singleton family returns its sole member; flattening the set of singleton images recovers the original set. The proofs use transpose reflection and cancellation respectively. For a family nested three levels deep, `associative` shows that flattening the inner families first gives the same result as flattening the outer family first: combine the image maps, apply the membership naturality square at membership itself, then split the composite image again.

[TransposeCounterexample](../src/AoP/TransposeCounterexample.agda) checks exercise 4.44. With the empty relation in place of a function, one side of the fusion equation returns the empty subset; the other has no output at all.

[InhabitedTranspose](../src/AoP/InhabitedTranspose.agda) checks exercise 4.45: a relation admits a transpose into inhabited subsets exactly when each input has an output. The transpose is unique up to equality of subset membership.

[SingletonTranspose](../src/AoP/SingletonTranspose.agda) checks exercises 4.46–4.47. Singleton formation is monic: testing two equal singletons at the first element identifies their elements. The image of a singleton is its fibre; the image of any subset is the union of the fibres at its members.

[FibreEquality](../src/AoP/FibreEquality.agda) proves exercise 4.48 with extensional equality of subsets. Two inputs have the same set of possible outputs exactly when each fibre contains the other. Left division expresses each containment. The witness for the shared set lives in the universe of predicates.

[PreorderFibres](../src/AoP/PreorderFibres.agda) checks exercise 4.49: `R` is a preorder exactly when it equals its left division by itself. Sending each input to its fibre realizes that preorder by inclusion of subsets, with extensional equality. Conversely, pulling a preorder back along any function preserves reflexivity and transitivity.

[FibreBounds](../src/AoP/FibreBounds.agda) checks exercise 4.50: an output belongs to a fibre exactly when every set containing the whole fibre contains that output. For the reverse direction, choose the fibre itself as the test set.

[TransformerOrder](../src/AoP/TransformerOrder.agda) checks exercise 4.51: a transformer is monotone exactly when postcomposition by it preserves pointwise inclusion. Constant transformers recover the original set inclusion in the reverse proof.

[LiberalPrecondition](../src/AoP/LiberalPrecondition.agda) checks exercise 4.52: a weakest liberal precondition requires every possible output to satisfy the postcondition. Sequential composition applies the two transformers in reverse order, and comparison on all postconditions reverses and reflects relation inclusion.

[TransformerEnvelope](../src/AoP/TransformerEnvelope.agda) handles the last part of 4.52 for an arbitrary predicate transformer `p`. At input `a`, keep precisely the outputs satisfying every postcondition accepted by `p` at `a`. Call this relation `S`. If `p ≤ wlp R`, each `R` edge must pass every such test, hence is an `S` edge. Thus `wlp S ≤ wlp R`. Conversely, `p ≤ wlp S` by construction, giving the reverse implication. Quantifying over small predicates puts `S` in `Set₁`; `Small` proves the original small-relation version given a logically equivalent small edge predicate. No monotonicity or global resizing is assumed.

The witness calculations above establish the laws in concrete `Rel`. Their derivations from abstract allegory axioms, and the full inventory of unnumbered examples remain open.
