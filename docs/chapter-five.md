# Chapter 5: relational datatypes

### §5.1: why a relator takes maps to maps

A map `f` in an allegory is entire and simple. Those conditions bound its two round trips: `id ⊑ f˘ ∘ f` and `f ∘ f˘ ⊑ id`. [RelatorMapConverse](../src/AoP/RelatorMapConverse.agda) transports the bounds through a monotone functor, using preservation of identity and composition. At this stage the transported right adjoint is `F(f˘)`. To identify it with `(F f)˘`, [AllegoryAdjointConverse](../src/AoP/AllegoryAdjointConverse.agda) proves Proposition 4.1 abstractly: apply the modular law to the unit to restrict the left arrow to its overlap with the converse of the right arrow; the counit shows the restriction still covers the whole left arrow. Reversing the adjunction gives the other inclusion. Consequently `F f` is a map and the two ways to form its converse agree (Lemma 5.1). Neither proof needs a tabulation.

[RelatorSpanConverse](../src/AoP/RelatorSpanConverse.agda) proves the forward direction of Theorem 5.1. Write `R = f ∘ g˘` using the maps from the rows of a table to the output and input. Reversing the relation swaps those maps, so `F(R˘)` reduces to `F g ∘ (F f)˘`. Reversing `F R` gives the same composite by the lemma and double converse. `Row.preserve` requires just the factorization equation and map legs; uniqueness of rows plays no role in this direction.

**Reverse direction: the mediator step needs a hypothesis.** The calculation contracts `F m ∘ (F m)˘` to an inclusion below identity, using simplicity of the image of the row map `m`. [ConverseFunctorWitness](../src/AoP/ConverseFunctorWitness.agda) tests this step on the finite category of decidable relations between the empty set and a singleton. [TwoObjectAllegory](../src/AoP/TwoObjectAllegory.agda) checks the source allegory laws and supplies a jointly monic table for each arrow. Empty arrows factor through zero rows and the single nonempty arrow through one row. The functor sends the two objects to a singleton and a Boolean pair; it sends empty arrows to full rectangles and the singleton identity to Boolean equality. This preserves identities, composition and converse. But the source empty relation is included in the singleton identity, whereas the full Boolean rectangle contains `(false,true)` and equality does not. In particular, the lifted empty-to-singleton row map has both outputs at its sole input. Converse preservation alone cannot supply that simplicity inequality here. [ConverseMediatorOrder](../src/AoP/ConverseMediatorOrder.agda) proves the conditional calculation. The smaller span has legs `f ∘ m` and `g ∘ m`; expanding its image exposes `F m ∘ (F m)˘` in the middle. If this lies below identity, the remaining factors form precisely the image of the larger span. The bound is needed only for the mediator in this comparison.

This inventory covers all 30 numbered Chapter 5 exercises. A relator lifts a relation on elements to one on structured values. [ExponentialRelator](../src/AoP/ExponentialRelator.agda), for example, relates two indexed families when their entries are related at every index. [RelationalProducts](../src/AoP/RelationalProducts.agda) relates pairs by independent choices in the two coordinates.

In §5.3, [InjectionTests](../src/AoP/InjectionTests.agda) checks the identities in exercise 5.12 for concrete relations. The left and right partial projections accept only the matching tag. Reinjecting their results recovers identity, and the union of the two branches recovers any copair. [InjectionAdjunction](../src/AoP/InjectionAdjunction.agda) derives the converse identities via Proposition 4.1 in concrete `Rel`. The matching-tag equations supply the units, and each reinjection lies below their union, which supplies the counits. The proposition then identifies each partial projection with the converse of its injection.

[CopairCancellation](../src/AoP/CopairCancellation.agda) checks equation 5.11 (exercise 5.13) in `Rel`. The intermediate point carries a tag, so its path belongs to exactly one branch of the construction. The result is the union of the two component composites. The relational tensor used for products has pairs of intermediate points; reversing arrows preserves that tensor rather than turning it into a tagged union.

[SumRestriction](../src/AoP/SumRestriction.agda) checks exercise 5.14: a step in the relational sum keeps its tag, so a common observation restricting that step need only be tested in the matching branch. Both inclusions retain the same observation witness.

In §5.4, [ImageNotRelator](../src/AoP/ImageNotRelator.agda) checks the obstruction in exercise 5.15. Adding the sole edge on a singleton changes the image of the full input from empty to full. The graph of the image function therefore loses its old output, violating monotonicity in the relation. Agreement on functions alone cannot identify it with the power relator.

[ImageDomainCover](../src/AoP/ImageDomainCover.agda) checks exercise 5.16. Passing the lifted domain test supplies an output for every input member. An output in the existential image already has an input witness. These two facts give both coverage conditions of the power relator.

[GuardCases](../src/AoP/GuardCases.agda) checks exercise 5.17 for concrete relations and decidable predicates. Each input receives exactly one branch tag. Refining a guarded conditional is equivalent to refining each branch on the inputs that reach it.

[ListMeet](../src/AoP/ListMeet.agda) checks the cons-list case of exercise 5.18. Two alignments of the same lists have matching constructors, so each pair of element witnesses can be combined into a witness for their intersection. [DatatypeMeet](../src/AoP/DatatypeMeet.agda) gives the calculation for a binary relation lifting on small sets, assuming meet preservation for one layer and the datatype’s computation and uniqueness laws in destructor form. Intersect the two lifted relations, unfold each at the same two endpoints, and use the layer law to combine their witnesses. The resulting relation satisfies the equation for the lift of the intersection; uniqueness gives both inclusions.

[EntireFold](../src/AoP/EntireFold.agda) checks the cons-list instance of exercise 5.19: an entire list algebra supplies a base value and a successor for each head and reduced tail, yielding a fold result for every input list. [EntireFoldReflection](../src/AoP/EntireFoldReflection.agda) gives the reflection calculation in concrete `Rel` for an arbitrary lifted endofunctor with the stated relator laws, fold computation equation and constructor reflection principle. Because the algebra accepts every input, the domain test of the fold commutes with the constructor in both directions. Reflection then gives `Id ⊆ dom H`, hence `Id ⊆ H ˘ ∘ H`.

[TransposeCombinations](../src/AoP/TransposeCombinations.agda) checks exercise 5.20. For a given input, the outputs of a union form the union of the two output sets; intersection works analogously. For independent inputs, the possible output pairs form the Cartesian product.

[ListSetify](../src/AoP/ListSetify.agda) checks exercises 5.21–5.22. Treating a list as its membership predicate turns nil into the empty set, a singleton list into a singleton set, cons into union with a singleton, and map into direct image. [SetifyCombination](../src/AoP/SetifyCombination.agda) checks the intermediate laws: append gives binary union, concatenation gives union over the member lists, and `cpr` pairs a fixed value with each member. Applying setification to the subsequence enumeration yields exactly the subsequence relation at the given input.

[SupersequenceFold](../src/AoP/SupersequenceFold.agda) checks exercise 5.23. The empty input permits any supersequence. Each required head permits an arbitrary prefix before its matching occurrence. The proof converts these witnesses in both directions to the skip/keep subsequence specification.

[ZippedSplits](../src/AoP/ZippedSplits.agda) checks the unnumbered split construction. [SuffixSnocLaw](../src/AoP/SuffixSnocLaw.agda) verifies the preceding last-element recurrence: append the new element to each old suffix, then include the empty suffix. [SuffixFold](../src/AoP/SuffixFold.agda) derives the descending suffix list by folding into a nonempty collection whose first entry is the entire input processed so far. Pair the prefixes in increasing length with these suffixes in decreasing length. The resulting list agrees in order and membership with the direct split enumeration, including both endpoint cuts.

[InitFold](../src/AoP/InitFold.agda) checks exercise 5.24: folding a nonempty list with an empty singleton result and a cons step removes exactly its last element. The proof reconstructs the input by appending the discarded element and shows that any such decomposition yields the same prefix.

[PartitionRanges](../src/AoP/PartitionRanges.agda) checks both range identities of exercise 5.25. Starting a new block produces precisely a singleton first block. Gluing produces precisely a first block with at least two elements. In either case all later blocks stay unchanged.

[PartitionFlatten](../src/AoP/PartitionFlatten.agda) checks exercise 5.26: the enumeration returns a list of nonempty blocks exactly when flattening those blocks gives the original input. The empty input has one partition, the empty list of blocks. [PartitionThree](../src/AoP/PartitionThree.agda) checks the book’s full example: the two gaps in `[1,2,3]` yield exactly four partitions, by cutting both, only the first, only the last, or neither. For a nonempty candidate the completeness proof removes either its first singleton block or the first element of a longer block, reconstructing the `new` or `glue` step.

[PartitionPredicate](../src/AoP/PartitionPredicate.agda) checks exercise 5.27: fold directly into a predicate on block lists. Each step takes the union of the `new` and `glue` images. Both directions of the proof identify that predicate with membership in the enumeration from exercise 5.26.

[PermuteTail](../src/AoP/PermuteTail.agda) checks the identity in exercise 5.28 for the insertion-based permutation relation: permuting the tail first leaves the set of final permutations unchanged. [BagTailCalculation](../src/AoP/BagTailCalculation.agda) gives the bag-based derivation assuming every bag has a representative list. It uses `bagify ∘ bagify˘ = id` to insert a tail representative, then the cons-fold equation to remove that intermediate list. [BagCountPermutation](../src/AoP/BagCountPermutation.agda) identifies the concrete insertion relation with equality of multiplicity functions for element types with decidable equality. In the reverse direction a positive count locates an occurrence; removing it cancels one increment on both sides and reduces the source list. The abstract bag laws alone do not assert this multiplicity characterization.

[InterleaveFold](../src/AoP/InterleaveFold.agda) checks exercise 5.29. A fold assigns each output entry to one of two lists while preserving order in each. Its converse agrees in both directions with consuming the next head of either input. The book’s example interleaves `[1,2,3,4]` and `[10,11,12]` to obtain `[1,10,2,3,11,12,4]`.

[LaxChoices](../src/AoP/LaxChoices.agda) checks exercise 5.30: wrapping one chosen output gives a cover of singletons; duplicating one output gives two compatible choices; taking the union of two covers gives a cover of the union. The unnumbered examples and intermediate calculations still need a full audit.
