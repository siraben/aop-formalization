## §10.2 Tab compression

[Detab](../src/AoP/Chapter10/Detab.agda) expands tabs to blanks at fixed tab stops. [EntabMinimum](../src/AoP/Chapter10/EntabMinimum.agda) proves that the buffered encoder produces a shortest representation at every positive width. [TabPrefixExample](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TabPrefixExample.agda) checks the width-eight example that rules out unrestricted prefix pruning: five glyphs and a tab encode eight columns in six characters, but the seven-column prefix requires at least seven characters. The exercises also examine alternative objectives and the intermediate loop derivation.

| Exercise | Calculation and scope |
| --- | --- |
| 10.1 | [ProjectedLoop](../src/AoP/Chapter10/ProjectedLoop.agda) proves the streaming loop equivalent to projecting the output of the accumulating loop. The invariant allows an arbitrary saved output prefix and column state; specializing to the empty prefix gives the exercise equation. |
| 10.2 | [TabCountObjective](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TabCountObjective.agda) checks a width-two counterexample. Both `TAB` and `BLANK TAB` expand to two blanks. One tab is the maximum over every valid encoding, yet the second encoding is longer. |
| 10.3 | [BlankTie](../src/AoP/Chapter10/BlankTie.agda) proves snoc-length monotonicity and refines the shortest encoder with a postprocessing pass: replace each tab spanning one column with a blank. Expansion and encoded length are preserved at every positive width, so shortestness transfers. |
| 10.4 | [FillPrefixes](../src/AoP/Chapter10/FillPrefixes.agda) proves the empty-input law for the fill-restricted prefix relation. For unrestricted prefixes it proves the empty and snoc laws and refutes the fill law at width one: the empty prefix of a filled empty line differs from the one-blank output. [FilledPrefix](../src/AoP/Chapter10/FilledPrefix.agda) proves the restricted fill law: filling again from a tab stop adds a whole width; a proper prefix starts shorter and can add at most a width. [FillLastBlank](../src/AoP/Chapter10/FillLastBlank.agda) proves the restricted snoc law. If a proper prefix and the extended line fill to the same output, the appended character must be a blank. Before the stop, removing that blank leaves the fill unchanged; at a stop, only the whole line qualifies. |

## §10.3 Minimum tardiness

[ScheduleCertificate](../src/AoP/Chapter10/ScheduleCertificate.agda) certifies the backward greedy algorithm against all permutations. [TardinessExample](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TardinessExample.agda) checks all six schedules of a three-job instance. The following entries separate the book’s calculations and variations from that correctness result.

| Exercise | Derivation or variant to audit |
| --- | --- |
| 10.5 | [ScheduleAlgebraOrder](../src/AoP/Chapter10/ScheduleAlgebraOrder.agda) proves monotonicity using job lists related by permutation as representatives of one bag. Their durations sum to the same time, so the appended job incurs the same penalty; maximum preserves the prefix cost comparison. |
| 10.6 | [ScheduleCostFactor](../src/AoP/Chapter10/ScheduleCostFactor.agda) proves (10.4) for empty and extended schedules: the cost is the maximum of the final job’s penalty and the prefix cost, with both components zero in the empty case. |
| 10.7 | [ScheduleInsertion](../src/AoP/Chapter10/ScheduleInsertion.agda) proves (10.7) by induction on the least snoc-insertion relation. Appending the new job preserves the previous maximum as a lower bound. When insertion occurs before an old last job, the strengthened invariant gives both a no-earlier completion time and a no-smaller prefix cost; monotonicity of the last penalty and maximum then give the result. |
| 10.8 | [BagAlgebraFibre](../src/AoP/Chapter10/BagAlgebraFibre.agda) derives both directions of (10.8) from the stated permutation-fold characterization and a representative for each bag. The empty bag has exactly the empty listing. A listing of a bag extended by `j` is obtained by inserting `j` into a listing of the original bag; `split` recovers such a listing and `join` reconstructs the bag equality. |
| 10.9 | [EqualWeightDeadline](../src/AoP/Chapter10/EqualWeightDeadline.agda) proves that among equal-weight jobs finishing at a common time, the one with the later due date incurs no greater penalty. Thus a latest-due job minimizes the final-position charge, including ties and zero weights. [DeadlineRun](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlineRun.agda) shows that every list built in nondecreasing due-date order at a common weight realizes a certified backward `Run`: at each prefix its last job minimizes the final charge. [DeadlineCertificate](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlineCertificate.agda) proves global optimality against every permutation of the original jobs: any due-date-ordered permutation at a common weight meets the scheduling specification, regardless of how ties were resolved. [DeadlineMerge](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlineMerge.agda) implements the two-run merge and records each due-date comparison. One comparison emits one job; draining the remaining run requires none. The comparison count is bounded by the output length. [DeadlineMergePermutation](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlineMergePermutation.agda) proves the merged output is a permutation of the concatenated runs: moving a right-hand head past the left run preserves that particular job occurrence. [DeadlineMergeOrder](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlineMergeOrder.agda) proves that merging two nondecreasing runs preserves due-date order: the chosen head is due no later than every remaining job on either side. [DeadlineMergeRound](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlineMergeRound.agda) pairs adjacent runs in one bottom-up pass and carries an unpaired run forward unchanged. The pass preserves every occurrence and keeps each output run sorted. Its total comparisons are bounded by the number of jobs across the output runs. [DeadlineRunCount](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlineRunCount.agda) proves that one pass takes `r` runs to exactly `⌈r/2⌉`; starting with at most `2^k` runs, `k` passes leave at most one. [DeadlinePassCost](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlinePassCost.agda) sums comparisons across the actual passes. Permutation preservation keeps the total job count `n` fixed, so `k` passes use at most `k*n` due-date comparisons. [DeadlineLog](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlineLog.agda) computes the least `k` with `n ≤ 2^k`: zero rounds for no jobs, and `⌈log₂ n⌉` for positive `n`. Five jobs require three rounds. [DeadlineSort](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlineSort.agda) starts with singleton runs, executes that many passes and returns a due-date-ordered permutation. The actual merge comparisons are bounded by `n*⌈log₂ n⌉` for positive `n`, with zero comparisons for empty input. [EqualWeightSchedule](../src/AoP/Chapter10/EqualWeightSchedule.agda) converts the sorted output to the last-job ordering certificate and returns `Solution jobs`: a permutation minimizing maximum weighted tardiness. The bound counts due-date comparisons; it excludes arithmetic on unbounded naturals and proof construction. [DeadlineSortExample](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DeadlineSortExample.agda) checks three jobs of weight 2. Sorting uses three comparisons and reduces maximum weighted tardiness from 10 to 2. |
| 10.10 | [SignedLateness](../src/AoP/Chapter10/SignedLateness.agda) evaluates maximum weighted lateness using signed penalties and a distinct bottom value for the empty schedule. Two orders of the same early-finishing jobs give peaks −3 and −2. [LatenessExchange](../src/AoP/Chapter10/LatenessExchange.agda) proves that earlier completion cannot increase a job’s signed penalty. The exchange argument therefore works at any integer threshold: if the chosen job fits the threshold in the last slot, moving it there preserves feasibility. [LatenessPick](../src/AoP/Chapter10/LatenessPick.agda) selects a final job of least signed penalty at the common finish time and returns its remaining occurrences. [LatenessSchedule](../src/AoP/Chapter10/LatenessSchedule.agda) repeats the signed selection at each remaining set of jobs, then appends the chosen occurrence. The returned schedule is a permutation of the input. [LatenessThreshold](../src/AoP/Chapter10/LatenessThreshold.agda) proves that for every integer ceiling, if any permutation meets it then the greedy schedule meets it. The competitor’s last job witnesses an admissible final penalty; minimum selection and exchange transfer the ceiling to the greedy order. [LatenessPeakBound](../src/AoP/Chapter10/LatenessPeakBound.agda) proves the score/threshold equivalence: an integer bounds the peak of a prefix followed by a suffix exactly when it bounds the prefix peak and each suffix job at its completion time. The empty prefix meets every integer ceiling. [LatenessOptimal.certified](../src/AoP/Chapter10/LatenessOptimal.agda) returns a permutation with globally minimum peak signed lateness. For a nonempty competitor, use its own finite peak as the integer ceiling and transfer that ceiling to the greedy run. For empty input the greedy peak is bottom. [LatenessChoiceExample](../src/AoP/Chapter10/LatenessChoiceExample.agda) runs both schedulers on two unit-duration jobs due at times 4 and 8. Both orders have zero tardiness; the signed solver puts the earlier deadline first and achieves peak −3, versus −2 for the tardiness solver’s tie choice. |
| 10.11 | [SumTardiness](../src/AoP/Chapter10/SumTardiness.agda) refutes the chapter’s backward minimum-final-penalty rule for the sum objective. Two jobs due at zero have durations 3 and 1 and weights 2 and 1. Selecting the short job last gives total 10; reversing them gives 9. This concerns that specific rule. |

## §10.4 TeX decimal output

[TeXExternal](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXExternal.agda) specifies shortest round-tripping output and verifies particular values. [TeXShortestSearch](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXShortestSearch.agda) gives an exhaustive reference printer. It searches all strings over the [ten digits](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXDigitAlphabet.agda) through length 17 and returns a shortest round-tripping string or a proof that none exists. The 17-digit cutoff sends every longer rival to a candidate of no greater length with the same decoded value. This finite search is impractically large. [TeXClosestShortest](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXClosestShortest.agda) refines this reference search: among strings of minimum length that decode to the target, it returns one with least absolute error. [TeXDistanceScale](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXDistanceScale.agda) checks the common denominator at fixed length and the scores for `.00007` and `.00008`. [TeXSinglePrinter](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXSinglePrinter.agda) certifies the single-loop strict-digit printer for the full TeX input range.

| Exercise | Remaining construction |
| --- | --- |
| 10.12 | [DigitInterval](../src/AoP/Chapter10/DigitInterval.agda) proves uniqueness for a nonnegative rational scaled fraction `n/w`, with positive denominator. After clearing denominators the digit must satisfy `d*w ≤ n < (d+1)*w`; two distinct digits would force `n < n`. |
| 10.13 | [TeXBoundedPrinter.extern](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXBoundedPrinter.agda) derives a two-pass integer printer for all `n < 65536`: zero prints the empty list; positive targets use the first feasible width and emit digits by strict division. Every output reads back as `n` and has minimum length among all rivals. The [calculation](tex-rounding.md) derives shortestness for the residual-controlled single loop by projecting every rival into the interval remaining after each digit. |
| 10.14 | [OpenUpperDigit](../src/AoP/Chapter10/OpenUpperDigit.agda) treats the excluded upper endpoint: for positive `10q` and `w`, choosing the floor of `(10q−1)/w` gives the unique `d` with `d*w < 10q ≤ (d+1)*w`, even at exact multiples. [StrictUpperQuotient](../src/AoP/Chapter10/StrictUpperQuotient.agda) derives both inequalities directly for Agda’s integer division primitive and computes the strict choice `4` at `10*65536 = 5*131072`. The [finite-domain audit](../checks/tex_printer_domain.py) finds no exact upper endpoint on any executed step for the 65536 TeX targets. A symbolic reachability proof and the full printer derivation remain open. |
| 10.15 | [TeXDistanceExample](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXDistanceExample.agda) compares two five-digit strings read as 5 scaled points: `.00007` and `.00008`. Multiplying both distances from `5/65536` by `100000 × 65536` gives 41248 and 24288, so `.00008` is closer. [TeXGreedyTieTrace](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXGreedyTieTrace.agda) computes an actual greedy output `.00016` with closer rival `.00015`. [TeXLoopGreatest](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXLoopGreatest.agda) proves that the strict-digit loop selects the greatest numerator below the open endpoint at the width it prints. Together with shortestness this identifies the greatest shortest candidate. The [fixed-width audit](tex-rounding.md) additionally checks that nearest rounding at the chosen width yields a closest candidate for every specified target. [TeXNearestNeighbours](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXNearestNeighbours.agda) proves that comparing the two grid points bracketing the target minimizes absolute error over all natural numerators. [TeXNearestClamp](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXNearestClamp.agda) proves that clamping a nearest numerator to any nonempty closed interval of integers minimizes error within that interval. [TeXNumeratorBounds](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXNumeratorBounds.agda) computes that interval from the half-open decoder bounds and a printable cap. [TeXNearestFeasible](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXNearestFeasible.agda) uses any feasible witness to select a minimum-error numerator there without enumerating rivals. [TeXRenderedNearest](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXRenderedNearest.agda) combines this selection with [fixed-width rendering](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXRenderWidth.agda), preserving the winning numerator, width and error certificate even when leading zeroes are required. [TeXClosestPrinter.extern](../src/AoP/Chapter10/TeXClosestPrinter.agda) applies this construction at the greedy printer’s shortest width for every target `n < 65536`. The resulting decimal decodes to `n`, has minimum length and minimizes absolute error among all round-tripping decimals of that length. The empty decimal handles zero. |
